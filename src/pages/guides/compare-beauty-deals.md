# Beauty Deals—Compare Sizes, Sets and Refills Before Buying

*Shopping With Noya · Sources checked November 8, 2026*

Start with the exact product, check how much you get, then compare what you will pay at checkout. A bigger bottle, a pretty gift set or a refill can be a good buy. The useful comparison is the version you want, in an amount you will use, with the packaging you need.

## Read the amount, not the bottle

Look for the net contents on the package or product details. FDA guidance distinguishes ounces by weight, written as “oz.” or “net wt.,” from fluid ounces, written as “fl. oz.”<a href="#source-1" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a> Grams measure weight; milliliters measure liquid volume.<a href="#source-2" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a>

Compare mL with mL, or grams with grams. Don’t treat a 1 oz. weight label as the same amount as 1 fl. oz. If both products list mL, use those numbers rather than converting their rounded fluid-ounce labels. The box size and the shipping weight aren’t the amount to put into your comparison.

## Match the formula before doing the math

Check the full name, shade, finish, scent and any stated concentration. Compare the same formula across sizes. A similar name or matching bottle color isn’t enough.

For example, The Ordinary lists Niacinamide 10% + Zinc 1% and Niacinamide 5% Face and Body Emulsion as separate products.<a href="#source-7" aria-label="Source 7">[7]</a><a href="#source-8" aria-label="Source 8">[8]</a> Treat them as different shopping choices, not small and large bottles of one formula. A lower cost per mL doesn’t tell you which formula to choose.

Also check for reformulations. The Ordinary notes that its formulas are updated and that a website ingredient list may differ from the box depending on purchase time and region.<a href="#source-7" aria-label="Source 7">[7]</a> If an older package and a newer listing don’t match, confirm the version before calling the comparison like-for-like.

## Full size or travel size?

Use the actual amount beside the selected size, rather than relying on “mini,” “travel” or “full size.” A small bottle can make sense for trying something new or carrying less. A larger bottle is worth comparing when you already want that much of the same product.

**Hypothetical example, not a current offer:** Imagine one cleanser formula sold at a fictitious retail price of $8 for 30 mL or $20 for 100 mL. Before tax and shipping:

- 30 mL bottle: $8 ÷ 30 = about $0.27 per mL.
- 100 mL bottle: $20 ÷ 100 = $0.20 per mL.

The larger bottle costs less per mL, but the smaller one costs less today. Choose based on whether you want the extra product. For your own comparison, divide the price by the amount and keep the units consistent.

## Open up the set’s contents list

A set is easier to judge when you write down each product and its amount. The Ordinary’s US Daily Set page, for example, lists a 50 mL Squalane Cleanser, a 30 mL Hyaluronic Acid 2% + B5 (with Ceramides), and a 30 mL Natural Moisturizing Factors + HA.<a href="#source-3" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a>

Those are different formulas. Adding their volumes together and dividing the set price by that total won’t show whether the cleanser is cheaper than buying it alone.

Instead, compare the set with buying the same components in the same amounts, where those options are available. Note which shades or products you actually want. If a pouch or brush is included, decide whether it is useful to you rather than automatically treating its advertised value as savings.

**Another hypothetical example:** A duo contains two 50 mL bottles of the same cleanser formula for a fictitious $18. One 100 mL bottle of that identical formula costs a fictitious $20. The duo is $0.18 per mL versus $0.20 per mL, or $2 less for the same amount, before tax and shipping. That calculation works because the contents match.

## Check what the refill fits

Read the refill instructions for the exact product and size. Check whether you need an existing jar, case or pump, and whether that part comes in the package you selected.

Charlotte Tilbury’s US 50 mL Magic Cream refill page says the refill fits the original Magic Cream jar.<a href="#source-4" aria-label="Source 4">[4]</a> That is a product-specific compatibility statement, not a reason to assume another brand’s cartridge will fit your container. Confirm the jar size and formula version too.

If you already own the compatible holder, compare the refill with the complete product at the same amount. If you need to buy the holder, include that cost in your first purchase comparison.

## Check the offer and the return route

Before paying, read who sells and ships the selected item. Keep one-time purchases separate from subscriptions, and check coupon eligibility in the order summary.

Ulta’s Replenish & Save page, for example, says orders are billed automatically and promo codes cannot apply to the initial subscription order.<a href="#source-5" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a> Don’t carry those terms over to a different retailer or subscription program.

Read the return terms for your purchase route, including the deadline, opened-product rules and what must come back with a set. Ulta’s policy says returns must include all original components and gives separate instructions for marketplace purchases.<a href="#source-6" aria-label="Source 6">[6]</a> Check the applicable policy rather than assuming every beauty purchase follows the same process.

## A quick pre-checkout checklist

- Exact formula, shade and concentration match what you want.
- Net amounts use the same units; each set component is accounted for.
- Any refill fits your container, or you have included the required holder.
- Seller, shipping charges and tax are clear.
- Purchase type and coupon conditions match the total shown.
- Quantity and return terms work for you.
